How this site works

The Lost Ledger

A short SQL adventure. The kingdom’s Royal Ledger is corrupted, and you’re Nish the Great, the new Spartan sent to fix it: explore the village, talk to the villagers, and open the Royal Archive’s rune doors with real SQL queries. Chapter 1 takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

Keyboard: click or use the arrow keys to move · Space to talk, read and use terminals · J to swing the Debugger · Esc for the menu · M for music. Phone: tap to move or use the on-screen arrows, A to talk and B to swing. Your progress saves automatically in this browser.

How to play

You are Nish the Great, a new Spartan at the Sparta Institute. The kingdom’s Royal Ledger is corrupted. Explore, talk to people, and open the rune doors by answering SQL questions about the kingdom’s data.

SQL terminals

Read the task, then type a query or tap the pieces to build it, and press Run (or Ctrl + Enter). If your results match, the rune glows: press Complete ✓. Extra columns are fine as long as the answer is there. A hint appears after two wrong tries, or straight away with a hint scroll. The Royal Ledger has four tables: Villagers (villager_id, name, job, house_id, age, coins), Houses (house_id, street, colour), Items (item_id, name, owner_id) and Monsters (monster_id, kind, room, hp).

Fighting

Bugs take two hits. NULL ghosts flicker: you can only hit them, and they can only hurt you, while they’re solid. Enemies only appear in the Royal Archive, drop keywords, and come back when you leave a room. Run out of hearts and you wake up at your last Ledger Stone.

Bram’s shop

Lantern of EXPLAIN (15) reveals the thin wall to a hidden room · Healing tea (3) refills your hearts · Hint scroll (5) shows a terminal’s hint straight away · Sharper Debugger (25): bugs in one hit, ghosts in two · Heart container (30).

Saving

The game saves as you go, and at every Ledger Stone (which also refills your hearts). Saves stay in this browser on this device; Continue on the title screen picks up where you left off.

             (cracked wall)          │        (hidden door)    (great door)

                       through the East Gate’s iron gate
  1. Sparta Institute. Talk to Trainer Ada. The front door is sealed until you answer the training terminal by the bookshelves on the left. Nudge: two columns from one table.
    Show answerSELECT name, job FROM Villagers;
    The seven bookshelves each hold an SQL tip.
  2. Village West. Elin the baker gives SQL advice; read the signpost and the noticeboard. Follow the path east.
  3. Village Square. Margaret, outside the library, gives you the Archive Pass. Wisp the ghost (top right) asks for help: his terminal is bottom right. Nudge: COALESCE fills in missing values.
    Show answerSELECT name, COALESCE(job, 'Unknown') FROM Villagers;
    Reward: a heart container. Bram’s stall is on the left; the Ledger Stone is in the middle of the crossroads.
  4. East Gate. Rex (left) asks you to count the villagers with no coins; his terminal is right of the path. Nudge: COUNT with a WHERE.
    Show answerSELECT COUNT(*) FROM Villagers WHERE coins = 0;
    Reward: 10 keywords. You should now have about 19: go back and buy the Lantern from Bram. Then show Odo your pass and the gate opens.
  5. Archive: Entrance. Touch the Ledger Stone. The Guard Rune terminal opens the north door. Nudge: WHERE with text in single quotes.
    Show answerSELECT name FROM Villagers WHERE job = 'Guard';
  6. North Hall. Watch for NULL ghosts. The chest (bottom left) holds the Index Map. The Elder Rune terminal (right) opens the east door. Nudge: sort, then keep one row.
    Show answerSELECT name FROM Villagers ORDER BY age DESC LIMIT 1;
  7. Reading Room (west of the North Hall). A chest holds 10 keywords. The cracked wall at the bottom opens with the Lantern, leading to the Hidden Vault: a heart container and a note from Margaret.
  8. East Hall (east of the North Hall). The Hoard Rune terminal (left) unlocks the chest (right) with the Primary Key. Nudge: GROUP BY and COUNT.
    Show answerSELECT owner_id, COUNT(*) FROM Items GROUP BY owner_id;
  9. Antechamber (south of the East Hall). A hidden door opens back to the Entrance, and there’s a Ledger Stone. The Bond Rune terminal, together with the Primary Key, opens the great door (east). Nudge: JOIN Items to Villagers on the owner.
    Show answerSELECT Items.name, Villagers.name FROM Items JOIN Villagers ON Items.owner_id = Villagers.villager_id;
  10. The Deadlock’s Chamber. Keep moving: the Deadlock keeps sending bugs. Each terminal releases one head’s lock.
    Left lock nudge: two JOINs, Items → Villagers → Houses.
    Show answerSELECT Houses.street FROM Items JOIN Villagers ON Items.owner_id = Villagers.villager_id JOIN Houses ON Villagers.house_id = Houses.house_id WHERE Items.name = 'Vault Rune';
    Right lock nudge: GROUP BY, then HAVING.
    Show answerSELECT room, COUNT(*) FROM Monsters GROUP BY room HAVING COUNT(*) > 2;
    Release both locks and the Ledger is restored.

    What's an ERD

    An entity relationship diagram is a map of a database. Each box is a table, and each line shows how two tables connect.

    Before writing any SQL against a database you haven't seen, look at its ERD. It tells you which tables exist, what columns they hold, and which columns you can use to join them together.

    The diagram on the right is the database you'll build in the Creating tables lesson: a Customers table and an Orders table, linked by customer_id.

    The four parts

    Entity

    A table, drawn as a box with its name in the header. Customers and Orders are entities.

    Attribute

    A column, listed inside the box with its data type on the right, such as customer_name TEXT.

    Primary key PK

    The column that uniquely identifies each row. No two customers can share a customer_id, and it can't be empty.

    Foreign key FK

    A column that holds another table's primary key. Orders.customer_id points at the customer who placed the order.

    What the lines mean: crow’s foot notation

    Each end of a relationship line carries a symbol, called crow's foot notation. The symbol next to a table says how many rows of that table can match one row at the other end.

    Read the example in both directions. Starting from Customers: one customer has zero or many orders, because a new customer may not have ordered yet. Starting from Orders: each order belongs to zero or one customer. It's "zero or one" because Orders.customer_id allows NULL. If that column were NOT NULL, the circle would become a second bar, meaning every order must have exactly one customer.
